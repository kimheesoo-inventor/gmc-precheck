Can't find "Request review" in Google Merchant Center?

Where Google's help pages say the button is, and the reasons they give for it being missing or greyed out. Check each one against what your own account shows.

Checked on 2026-10-30 against the Google help pages linked in the text. Google's menus and wording change, so if something here doesn't match your screen, open the linked page. Nothing here is a guarantee: Google makes the decision.

1. Where the button normally is

In Merchant Center (Google's review page):

  1. Go to Products.
  2. Select the Needs attention tab above the summary cards.
  3. Select View setup and policy issues.
  4. For the issue, select I fixed the issue (or I disagree with the issue), then Request review.

Google's Shopify guide also names Review and fix on the Home page, Fix on the banner at the top of the account, and Fix on a card in the Needs attention tab. Google says the option shows on the product detail page or in the list of issues "if available", so it isn't offered for every issue.

In the Google & YouTube app on Shopify (same Shopify guide): in Shopify admin go to Sales channels > Google & YouTube > Settings, and under your Google account select Request review. A form opens on a Google Merchant Help page; complete it, say you're requesting a review of your store, and submit.

2. Reasons it can be missing or greyed out

  1. A cool-down period. Google says the review button is disabled (greyed out) during a cool-down. It can start after unsuccessful reviews; for issues you fixed, the review page says one may begin if issues aren't resolved by the second review attempt. The end date is in Needs attention > View setup and policy issues. Google says support can't bypass or shorten it, and it may get longer with each unsuccessful review. Google says to use this time to audit your store and check that your product data matches it.
  2. Merchant verification not finished. Google says you may need to complete other actions first, such as merchant verification. Check the Needs attention tab for requests to submit a government-issued ID or business documents. For Misrepresentation suspensions, Google says this must be done before requesting a review.
  3. An empty product data source. If your data source has no products, Google says to manually add at least one product in All products under Products to become eligible for a review.
  4. A suspended Google Ads account is linked. Google's linked account suspension page says the Google Ads suspension must be fixed before the Merchant Center suspension can be reviewed, and that unlinking the Ads account won't resolve it. Once Ads is unsuspended, request the Merchant Center review if it's still suspended.
  5. Google thinks your account was compromised. Google's compromised account page says that in this case it suspends the Merchant Center account and also disables the Google account. The steps it lists are: recover your Google account, report your compromised Merchant Center account, and tell your account manager if you have one. That page doesn't describe a Request review button for this case.

3. If your website itself was hacked

Google's Abuse of the network policy covers "compromised sites": code changed to benefit someone else without the owner knowing, such as injected scripts, malware, pop-ups or redirects, or a content management system with known security vulnerabilities that has been exploited.

  • Remove the injected code and fix the weakness that let it in, such as an outdated content management system or plugin.
  • If your site is verified in Google Search Console, open the Security issues report. Fix every issue it lists on every page, then select Request Review there and describe what you fixed. Google's review guide on web.dev says reviews for sites hacked with spam can take up to several weeks.
  • Then, if Merchant Center still shows the issue, request a review there as in section 1.

Google's pages don't say whether a Search Console review changes a Merchant Center suspension, so treat them as two separate requests.

4. Still stuck

  • Google's review page says to contact support if you're uncertain about the issue or how to proceed.
  • In the Google & YouTube app on Shopify, Google's Shopify guide says to use the 3-dot menu at the top right and select Get support.
  • Review times differ between Google's pages: the review page says to wait 7 business days and that a review may take up to 2 weeks; the Shopify guide says reviews typically take up to 3-7 business days.

5. If the suspension is for Misrepresentation

Google's review page lists checks to do before requesting a review: business details such as address, phone and email visible on your store and matching your Merchant Center settings, return and shipping policies that work and are clear, and product data that matches your store.
