Shopify + Google Merchant Center Misrepresentation: a store-page checklist before you request a review

If Google suspended your Merchant Center account for Misrepresentation, fix everything you can find on your public store pages first, then ask for a review. This checklist links what Google's help pages ask for to where you change it in Shopify admin.

Checked on 2026-10-29 against the Google help pages linked in the text and the Shopify help pages listed at the bottom of this page. Shopify's menus can change, so if a name here doesn't match your admin, open the matching Shopify help page. Nothing here is a guarantee: Google makes the decision.

1. What Google asks of your store's website

Google's Misrepresentation policy says listings and destinations that deceive people, by leaving out relevant information or giving misleading information about products and businesses, aren't allowed. Examples it gives that show up on store pages:

  • A false business name or false contact information.
  • Hiding or misrepresenting your real business location, or where your products ship from.
  • Not showing the total price, or all shipping costs, including return shipping costs in some cases.
  • Hiding fulfillment times or refund restrictions until after the customer commits, or denying a return your own policy allows.

For stores using the Google & YouTube app, Google's Shopify guide lists what to audit for Misrepresentation: consistent business details, linked shipping, refund and privacy policies, no placeholder text or broken links, a working HTTPS checkout, and identity verification. Google's page on building trust also says to avoid placeholders because they make a site feel unfinished.

2. Step-by-step: what to check and where to fix it

  1. Contact details. In Shopify admin go to Settings > General > Store contact details. Check the store name, the store address (company name and address) and the phone number. Your Store email is also in Settings > General. Shopify says this information is public on your store and policy pages. Google wants your legal business name, physical address, phone number and email clearly listed and the same as in your Merchant Center settings. Compare with Settings > Business info in Merchant Center. Use your real business address: Google lists "displaying a false or virtual physical address as your headquarters while operating entirely from an undisclosed region" as a Misrepresentation example.
  2. Written policies. Go to Settings > Policies and open each one: return and refund, shipping, privacy, terms, contact information. Read them as a customer would. If Use automated policy is on, Shopify says to still review the result, because it isn't guaranteed to be complete.
  3. Policy links in the footer. Go to Content > Menus and open the menu your footer uses. You can add a menu item and choose Policies as the link type. Then click every footer link on the live store, not only in admin. Google asks that contact information, shipping policies and return and refund details be "clear, consistent, and easily accessible in your website's footer", and suggests a footer button linking to the return and refund policy.
  4. Leftover template text. Look for things like [INSERT RETURN ADDRESS] or [LINK TO REFUND POLICY]. Check each policy, your pages (Online Store > Pages, such as About and Contact), and the theme editor (Online Store > Themes > Edit theme). Shopify's Edit default theme content screen (in the menu next to your theme) has a search box, but it covers your theme's built-in wording. Text you typed into sections or pages is better found in the theme editor and Pages. On the live pages, use your browser's Find (Ctrl+F or Cmd+F) for [, example, 123 and lorem.
  5. Sample contacts. Search for support@example.com, +1 (123) 456-7890, a mistyped email ending such as .ccom, or an address that isn't yours. These can sit in the footer, a contact page or a theme block.
  6. Social links. In the theme editor, Shopify's help says social media links are in Theme settings. Also check the footer section itself. Click each icon on the live store and make sure it goes to your own profile, not a sample or someone else's account; remove any you don't use. Google suggests linking your own social profiles.
  7. Same details everywhere. Google says your business name, address and contact details should match across your website, Merchant Center settings and payment profiles.
  8. Working pages. Fix broken links and placeholder images, and make sure your checkout works and is secure (the address starts with https://).

These are examples seen on public Shopify stores, not a list of what Google flagged for any particular account, and not a measure of how common they are.

3. Shipping and returns wording

Shipping. Rates and delivery times are set in Settings > Shipping and delivery; fulfillment time is under Estimated delivery dates there. Check that:

  • Shipping costs are easy to find before checkout and match your shipping policy.
  • A fast delivery claim doesn't hide a longer processing time elsewhere. Google lists that as a problem, and Shopify says not to promise delivery times you can't provide.
  • Where you ship from is stated honestly.

Returns. Return window, return shipping and restocking fee are set in Settings > Policies > Return and cancellation rules. Check that:

  • The written policy states the return window or other conditions, and who pays return shipping. If you don't accept returns, say so clearly (Google's fix page).
  • The wording is the same in the footer link, the returns page and any banner.
  • Any restocking fee is stated before checkout.
  • For international orders, the return window doesn't start on the purchase date when you mean delivery. Google lists that as a problem. In Shopify the Starting from setting controls it.
  • If a returns app handles returns, the policy matches what the app does.

4. Before you click "Request review"

Fix everything first. Google's review page says that if your issues aren't resolved by the second review attempt, a one-week cool down period may begin. During it the review button is disabled, the period may increase with each unsuccessful review, and Google support can't bypass or shorten it. So one careful request is better than several quick ones.

  • Update your product data in Merchant Center, or wait for your upload schedule to run, before requesting (Misrepresentation policy).
  • If you fixed the problem, choose I fixed the issue. Google says you may only have one chance to choose I disagree with the issue.
  • With the Google & YouTube app: Sales channels > Google & YouTube > Settings, then Request re-review under your Google account. Otherwise use the Needs attention tab in Merchant Center.
  • Google says a review may take up to 7 business days, and may take longer for complex reviews. That is Google's statement, not a promise from this page.

What to write in the review notes: list only what you actually changed, with the page and the change, for example "Replaced the sample email in the footer with our support address". Don't claim anything you haven't done, and don't argue the policy. Don't use a made-up address, fake reviews or a new account to get around a suspension; false business details are themselves listed by Google as Misrepresentation.

5. What this checklist cannot see

  • Identity verification. Google says to check Needs attention for a request to submit ID, and to complete it before requesting a review.
  • Product data vs. your site. Prices and availability in your product data must match your pages exactly.
  • Inside Merchant Center and Shopify admin. The checklist is about public pages. Your account's own notice and email show Google's stated reason. Use those first.
