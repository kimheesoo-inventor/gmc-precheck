Google Merchant Center "Misrepresentation" suspensions: what 119 public threads show

We collected 119 public posts from the Google Merchant Center Community that matched the search words "suspended" or "misrepresentation" (created 2026-07-13 to 2026-10-28). Some are not about a Merchant Center suspension. Rough takeaway: when we scanned 12 Shopify stores from these posts, only 2 still showed template text in their terms of service. Among the posts that mention misrepresentation, the most common keyword topics were contact or address details, return, shipping or refund policy, and identity verification.

This is a small, rough count of public posts, not a study of how often each cause happens. Data collected 2026-10-30. Nothing here is a guarantee: Google makes the decision.

How we counted

  • We used the public Community lists filtered for "suspended" and "misrepresentation". After removing repeats, that gave 119 unique threads.
  • 76 of the 119 posts mention misrepresentation in the title or first post.
  • Platform: we looked at today's homepage of the store URL written in the post. If there was no URL, we used a platform name written in the post.
  • Topics: we matched keywords. This is not a manual read-through of every post.

Limits: public posts only, so stores whose owners never posted are not counted. A store's platform today may differ from when it posted. 83 of the 119 threads have no platform we could identify. The sample is small. Shopify may look bigger than it is because owners tend to name it more often.

What our checker found on 12 suspended Shopify stores

We ran our free page checker on the 12 Shopify stores whose owners posted their store URL, starting from each homepage (the tool also opens the store’s own policy pages). We are not naming any store or post.

  • 2 of 12 still had template text in their terms of service, for example [LINK] and [insert email/contact info].
  • The other 10 showed no template or sample-text problem that the tool can see.

Two caveats. We checked the pages on 2026-10-30, after the posts, so owners may have already fixed things. And a page scan cannot see account-level causes, such as an identity check or a mismatch inside Merchant Center. Notes like "no email or phone on the homepage" are weak signals, because that information may be on a contact page.

What to check before you request another review

Google's Misrepresentation policy gives examples such as false contact information, hiding your real business location, and not showing all shipping costs. Your own notice is the best guide to your case. This list sticks to what Google's help pages say.

  • Read Google's emails and notice. Google says you will receive emails with the details of the policy violation and the suspension. See Google's guide for Shopify.
  • Check the "Needs attention" tab for an ID request. If Google asks for a government-issued ID, Google says this must be done before you request a review. See Google's help page on reviews.
  • Make your business details match. Google says business details such as address, phone and email should be visible on your store and match your Merchant Center settings (same help page). Its Shopify guide also asks for your legal business name, address, phone and email to be clearly listed (Shopify guide).
  • Remove placeholders and broken links, and link your policies. Google asks you to remove placeholder text, fix broken links, and link to your shipping, refund and privacy policies (Shopify guide).
  • Make your return and refund policy easy to find and consistent. Google says to state it clearly, say so if you do not accept returns, and keep it the same in the footer, on the returns page and in any banner (Google's return policy page).
  • Match product data to your site. Google says prices, availability and product attributes in your data should match your website exactly, and to update the data or wait for your upload schedule before you request a review (reviews help page, policy page).
  • Do not rush the request. Google says that if issues are not resolved by the second review attempt, a one-week cool down may begin, support cannot shorten it, and it may get longer after each unsuccessful review. Google's pages give different review times, from about 7 business days up to 2 weeks (reviews help page), so treat any timing as approximate.

Our own advice, not Google's: fix everything you can find first, then send one careful request.
